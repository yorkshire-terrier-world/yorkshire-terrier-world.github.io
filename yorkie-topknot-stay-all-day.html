How to Make a Yorkie Topknot Stay All Day: Bands, Bows and a Daily Routine That Prevents Mats

The bands that actually grip fine Yorkie hair, how tight to tie it, and a five-minute daily routine that keeps the topknot up and the coat mat-free.

Published 2026-11-08

A Yorkie topknot that slides down over the eyes by lunchtime is one of the most common small frustrations of living with a long-coated Yorkshire Terrier. The good news is that a topknot that stays put is rarely about a magic product. It comes down to clean, tangle-free hair, the right band in the right spot, and a short daily reset that keeps the coat healthy and your dog's eyes clear and comfortable.

Quick answer: brush the head fall completely tangle-free, part a triangle from the outer corner of each eye back to the small bump on top of the head, and secure it with soft latex topknot bands that sit snug but never pull the skin. Add the bow after the first band, not instead of it. Take the topknot out every evening (cut the band, don't drag it), brush through, and put a fresh one in each morning. That daily redo is what stops mats from forming at the base.

Why does a Yorkie topknot fall out?

Most topknots fall out because the hair was not fully brushed out first, the band is the wrong type, or the section is too thin to grip. Fine, silky Yorkie hair is slippery, so anything tied over tangles or with a slick elastic loosens as soon as the dog shakes or rubs its head.

The topknot exists for a practical reason. The Yorkshire Terrier's head fall grows long and drops straight into the face. As the Yorkie Info Center topknot guide explains, the bow keeps that hair out of the water bowl, out of the food and, most importantly, out of the eyes. A topknot that holds all day is really about your dog's comfort: clear vision, a dry face after drinking and less hair rubbing at the eye surface.

The three usual culprits

  • Slippery hair. Freshly washed, conditioned silky coat has very little grip. A band that holds on a day-old coat can slide off one that was bathed an hour ago.
  • Too little hair in the section. If you only gather the very top of the fringe, the weight of the bow drags it forward. The section needs to start at the outer corners of the eyes.
  • Wrong fastener. Smooth fabric elastics and clip-only bows have nothing to lock onto in fine hair, so they creep down with every head shake.

Why tangles make it worse

A topknot tied over a small knot sits on an uneven base. The band rocks, the hair around the knot twists, and within a day or two you have a felted lump right where the band sits. That is why brushing first is the single biggest factor in the checker above.

Bands, bows or clips: what works best for a Yorkie topknot?

Soft latex topknot bands are the most reliable base, with the bow added on top of the first band. Clips and barrettes look easy but slip in fine Yorkie hair, and plain rubber or office bands grip so hard they snap hairs when you remove them.

The American Kennel Club notes that bows are actually part of the Yorkie breed standard, which says "the fall on the head is long, tied with one bow in center of head or parted in the middle and tied with two bows." You don't need show-ring perfection at home, but the standard explains why the bow sits in the center of the head, and why the band, not the bow, does the holding.

A note on safety

The same Yorkie Info Center guide warns that cheap bows and ties slip off or break apart and can become a choking hazard, especially for puppies that mouth anything that falls on the floor. It also suggests latex-free materials, avoiding wire-framed ribbons, and checking decorated bows for loose rhinestones. If your dog has ever reacted to latex, switch to soft coated elastics. Whatever you use, check the floor and your dog's bed when a bow goes missing.

How do you make a Yorkie topknot that stays all day?

Brush and comb the head fall until a comb passes through without catching, part a triangle from the outer corner of each eye back to the bump on top of the head, band it in two sections, then add the bow and join both sections with a final band just above it. The steps below follow the method described in the Yorkie Info Center guide, with notes on what actually makes it hold.

Step 1: Start with a tangle-free head fall

Work through the hair on the head with a pin brush, then follow with a fine comb. If the comb catches, stop and loosen that spot with your fingers before going on. Brushing toward the skin over a knot only tightens it. A light mist of water or a leave-in coat spray reduces static and breakage, but too much product makes the hair slippery, so go easy on the morning of the topknot.

Step 2: Part a clean triangle

Use the tail of a comb to draw a line from the outer corner of one eye back to the small bump at the center of the skull, then the same on the other side. Everything inside that triangle goes into the topknot. Taking hair all the way from the eye corners is what keeps it from flopping forward, because the hair that drops into the eyes is now anchored.

Step 3: Band in two sections

Split the triangle into a front section and a back section. Put a band on the front section about an inch (2.5 cm) from the skin, then hold a few hairs from the center and slide the band down toward the head until it sits snug. Repeat for the back section. Two smaller bands grip fine hair far better than one large one.

Step 4: Add the bow, then lock it

Place the bow on the front section. Then gather both sections and join them with one final band slightly above the bow. The bow ends up sandwiched, so it cannot slide forward, and the topknot keeps its shape even if your dog shakes hard.

Step 5: Check comfort before you let go

Gently push the topknot from side to side. Your dog should be able to blink and close the eyes normally, the skin of the forehead should not look stretched, and there should be no scratching at the band. If any of those fail, take it out and redo it a little looser.

How tight should a Yorkie topknot be?

Snug enough not to slide, loose enough that the skin of the forehead stays relaxed and your Yorkie can close its eyes completely. If the eyebrows look lifted or the dog paws at its head after you finish, the band is too tight.

A tight topknot is one of the most common reasons a dog fights grooming. It pulls on sensitive skin all day, and many dogs scratch or rub until the whole thing comes out, which looks like a hold problem but is really a comfort problem. The fix is in step 3: leave the band off the skin, then ease it down while holding the center hairs so the scalp is never pulled.

Signs the topknot is too tight

  • Your dog cannot fully close its eyes, or the eyes look wider than usual.
  • Repeated head shaking, pawing or rubbing the face on the floor right after tying.
  • Redness, flaking or broken hairs at the base when you take it out in the evening.

Signs it is too loose

  • The topknot leans or swivels when you touch it.
  • Strands fall out around the eyes within an hour or two.
  • The bow migrates to one side of the head.

The daily topknot routine that prevents mats

A long Yorkie coat tangles fast. Wikipedia's Yorkshire Terrier entry describes the traditional long coat as extremely high maintenance and notes that it may knot if it is not brushed daily, with a pin brush recommended for long coats. The head fall is no exception. The base of a topknot that stays in for days is exactly where hair twists into mats, so the most reliable routine is a short daily reset.

Morning (about 5 minutes)

  1. Brush the head fall with a pin brush, then comb it through with a fine comb.
  2. Check the corners of the eyes and wipe away any debris so it does not get tied in.
  3. Part the triangle, band the two sections, add the bow and the joining band.
  4. Do the comfort check: blinking, relaxed skin, no pawing.

Through the day

  1. After meals and drinks, glance at the topknot and the face hair below it.
  2. After rough play or a walk through grass, check for burrs or twigs caught in the bands. If you take your Yorkie on longer outings, our guide on how far a Yorkie can hike covers trail days, and a quick topknot check belongs at the end of every one.

Evening (about 3 minutes)

  1. Remove the bow, then cut the bands out with small blunt scissors (more on this below).
  2. Brush and comb the head fall gently from the ends toward the skin.
  3. Leave the hair down overnight so the skin rests, unless the hair falls into your dog's eyes while sleeping, in which case use one loose band.

Weekly

Wash the face and head fall on bath day and let the coat dry fully before the next topknot, since freshly washed silky hair is at its most slippery. If you are unsure how often the whole coat needs a bath, our guide to how often to bathe a Yorkie explains the trade-off between a clean coat and a dried-out one. Also check your bands and bows for fraying, and throw out any that are worn.

How do you take a topknot out without breaking hair?

Cut the band rather than pulling it. Slide the tip of a small pair of blunt-tipped scissors or a band cutter under the band, away from the skin, and snip, then tease the band out with your fingers.

Pulling a latex band off drags it along the hair shaft and takes broken hairs with it every time. Over weeks, that leaves a short, frizzy patch right at the front of the head, which then sticks out of every new topknot and makes the whole thing look messier. Cutting is quicker and kinder to the coat.

A safe way to cut a band

  • Hold the topknot up and away from the head so you can see the band clearly.
  • Point the scissors away from the eyes and skin, and cut only the band, at the top of the topknot.
  • Unwind the cut band with your fingers, then comb through.
  • If the band has worked into a tangle, loosen the tangle first with your fingers and a little coat spray.

If your dog wriggles, have a second person hold and reward, or do it when your Yorkie is sleepy after a walk. Never cut near the eyes with a dog that is moving.

Puppy topknots: teaching a young Yorkie to keep it in

Puppies are the hardest topknot clients. Their hair is shorter and softer, the section is small, and they often scratch at anything new on their head. Expect the first topknots to come out, and treat it as a handling lesson rather than a grooming task.

Start small and positive

  • Use just one small band on the front section at first, with no bow.
  • Give a treat right after it goes in, then distract with play or a meal so your puppy forgets about it.
  • If the puppy works it out, wait and try again later. Don't scold.
  • Never leave a puppy unsupervised with a bow or clip on, because of the choking risk mentioned above.

Short sessions build patience the same way crate or pen training does. When a short cut makes more sense

Not every Yorkie needs a topknot. Wikipedia notes that many owners keep the coat trimmed short for easier care, and plenty of pet groomers will trim the hair between the eyes so it stays out of the face without bands. If your dog truly hates the topknot or you can't keep up the daily routine, a short head trim is a perfectly healthy choice.

Common Yorkie topknot mistakes

  • Tying over tangles. The base rocks, the hair twists and a mat starts right under the band.
  • Leaving the same topknot in for days. This is the most common route to a matted lump at the base.
  • Using office rubber bands. They grip so strongly that removing them snaps hair.
  • Pulling the band off instead of cutting it. Every pull leaves broken hairs behind.
  • Pulling the skin tight. Uncomfortable topknots get scratched out, and tension irritates the skin.
  • Gathering too little hair. If the section doesn't start at the eye corners, strands fall straight back into the eyes.
  • Relying on a clip on fine hair. Clips slide, and a loose clip on the floor is a choking hazard.
  • Tying the topknot on wet or just-conditioned hair. Let it dry fully first, or the band slides.

Frequently asked questions about Yorkie topknots

How long can a Yorkie topknot stay in?

For a healthy coat, redo it every day. Take it out in the evening, brush through and put a fresh one in the next morning. Topknots left in for several days are the most common cause of mats at the base.

What bands are best for a Yorkie topknot?

Soft latex topknot bands hold fine Yorkie hair best and are what show groomers commonly use. If your dog is sensitive to latex, or the hair is thick and cottony, try soft coated elastics. Avoid plain rubber or office bands, which break hair.

Why does my Yorkie keep scratching out the topknot?

Usually it is too tight, or it is new and the dog isn't used to it yet. Check that the forehead skin is relaxed and your dog can close its eyes fully. With puppies, start with one small band and reward right after it goes in.

Is a topknot required for a Yorkie?

Only in the show ring. The AKC breed standard calls for the head fall to be tied with one bow in the center of the head or parted and tied with two bows. At home, you can just as well keep the hair between the eyes trimmed short.

Can my Yorkie sleep with a topknot in?

It is kinder to let the hair down overnight so the skin rests and you can brush the base. If the hair falls into the eyes at night, a single loose band is fine, but take it out and brush through in the morning.

How do I get mats out of a Yorkie's topknot?

Work small mats loose with your fingers and a little detangling or coat spray, then use a comb from the ends toward the skin. If a mat sits tight against the skin, don't cut near the skin yourself; ask a groomer to remove it safely.

The short version

A Yorkie topknot stays all day when it is built on brushed-out hair, anchored from the eye corners, held with soft latex bands in two small sections and finished with the bow locked between bands. Keep the skin relaxed, cut bands out instead of pulling, and redo it every morning. That small daily habit keeps the coat free of mats and your Yorkie's eyes clear and comfortable.
